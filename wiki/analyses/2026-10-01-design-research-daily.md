## Design Research

Bento grids continue as a 2026 standard, but the innovation shift is toward "active" tiles that expand or reveal on interaction—essential for dashboards with dense information. Habit trackers confirm that minimalist streak mechanics (Streaks app) match Mission Control's current identity better than full RPG systems, while neumorphic depth (with high-contrast safeguards) offers tactile affordance without abandoning the retro JRPG look.

### 1. Active Bento Grid: Interactive Tiles Over Static Cards

In 2026, bento grids have evolved from static layouts into "active grids" where tiles are highly interactive—hovering over them expands them, plays video, or reveals secondary data layers. Mission Control's current quest/calendar/wallet cards are static; adding subtle expand-on-hover states (task details, event peek, breakdown sparkline) could deepen engagement without breaking the grid structure or adding new rows.

### 2. Streak Over Points: Minimalist Chain Visualization for Retention

Streaks (Apple's App of the Year 2021) uses a single-chain visualization as the most successful minimal gamification mechanic in habit tracking, producing strong retention among users who want simplicity. Mission Control's day-streak counter is already in place; emphasizing the unbroken chain visually (a growing counter with a single satisfying animation on mark) aligns better with user feedback patterns (past ratings show "Not even close" on complex gamification) than adding badges or levels.

Source: [Gamification in Habit Tracking Apps: Examples and Results](https://guul.games/blog/gamification-in-habit-tracking-apps-examples-and-results)

### 3. Neumorphic Depth as Affordance: Inset Shadows for Clarity Without Losing Retro Style

Neumorphism uses inset and outset shadows to create depth: inset shadows make elements look pressed into the surface, with paired light and dark shadows adding subtle depth while staying minimal. Mission Control already has hard-shadow JRPG borders; swapping or layering in subtle inset shadows on interactive cards (quest rows, event blocks, input fields) can signal affordance—what can be clicked or toggled—without breaking the retro aesthetic. Pair with adequate contrast per WCAG to avoid the neumorphism trap.
