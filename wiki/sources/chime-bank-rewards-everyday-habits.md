# Bank rewards are shifting from big balances to everyday habits

**Author:** Chime Prime (paid placement in USA Today's Sponsor Story section, published 2026-10-14)
**Source:** `raw/chime-bank-rewards-everyday-habits.md`
**Ingested:** 2026-10-26

> [!warning] Sponsored content
> [[chime]] paid for this advertorial, and every survey figure comes from a survey Chime commissioned. Treat it as marketing that promotes Chime's own product, not independent reporting. No independent source in the wiki corroborates the figures yet.

## Summary

The piece argues that bank rewards are moving away from large balances and toward routine behavior, especially payroll direct deposit. Chime calls the resulting product category the [[rewards-checking-account]]: cash back or a higher savings rate for simply using a checking account, with no annual fee. It says public perception has not caught up, since most people still think top perks need a high balance or a fee-based rewards credit card.

The article uses a Chime-commissioned survey to show this perception gap, then presents Chime Prime as the example. Chime Prime unlocks rewards through $3,000+ in monthly direct deposits rather than through a minimum balance.

## Quantitative Data

**Survey:** commissioned by Chime and conducted by Talker Research. n = 2,000 Americans with checking accounts.

## Key Takeaways

- Eligibility for perks is shifting from a **wealth proxy** (balance) to a **behavioral proxy** (consistent direct deposit).
- Chime presents a rewards checking account as an alternative to an annual-fee rewards credit card.
- The survey's methodology (sampling, question wording) is not disclosed. Only n = 2,000 is given.
- The piece itself says traditional banks may never adopt this model.

## Key Claims
