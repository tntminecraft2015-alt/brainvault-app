DESIGN RESEARCH · BY RED

Calendar Upgrades & Gamification for Mission Control

Found three high-impact calendar patterns from 2025–2026 design trends: heatmap overlays that turn calendar cells into data-rich insights, tile-based color-coding for visual streak motivation (vs. pure numerics), and adaptive event density via '+N more' popover patterns. All three fit Mission Control's existing bento-card JRPG aesthetic and can deepen the calendar's role as a visual feedback loop for habit/task progress.

3 findings · focus: Calendar upgrades and designs

FINDING 1 / 3

Data-Rich Calendar Heatmaps (Dual-View Pattern)

Calendar interfaces can overlay heatmaps showing financial or contextual performance alongside event scheduling, giving users both scheduling and strategic overviews at a glance. For Mission Control, this means toggling between a standard monthly grid and a heatmap showing XP earned per day, task completion density, or event-kind frequency. Mission Control's rank colors (S/A/B/C) and event kind chips already support this visual layering. A heatmap toggle in the calendar modal lets players see patterns at a glance.

Source: Eleken Blog — Calendar UI Examples

FINDING 2 / 3

Tile-Based Streak Grid (Gamification via Color, Not Just Numbers)

Habit trackers using tile-based grids make tracking visually intuitive and satisfying, transforming habit tracking into an engaging experience. Apps like HabitBull visualize streaks by highlighting successful days in green and missed days in pink, acknowledging that life can interfere with streaks while still providing a positive overall view. Mission Control's event-kind chips (training/meeting/focus/social/errand/routine) could fill calendar cells with color blocks instead of text event titles, making a full month of colored tiles feel like filling a progress grid—pure visual satisfaction.

Source: Habit Rewards & Gamification+ — Habit Tracker Design

FINDING 3 / 3

Overflow Management with '+N More' Popovers & Expand Toggle

The standard calendar overflow approach shows 2–3 events with a '+N more' indicator—clicking it reveals a popover or expands the cell. Mission Control's current monthly grid already hints at event overflow; implementing a popover for dense days lets high-activity calendars remain scannable while hiding nothing. Event styling should communicate category and duration through color and icons, especially in high-density calendars where color-coding must include pattern or icon differentiation for color-blind users.

Source: Muzli — Calendar UI/UX Inspiration
